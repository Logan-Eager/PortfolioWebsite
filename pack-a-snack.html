PACK A SNACK

Introduction

The Project

This project was a rewarding group-based, client-led project. Which became a 2D, 2-player Unity game with several engaging mini-games, and a focus on nutritional education. We were given a total of 6 weeks to make a vertical-slice of a fleshed out game prototype, (Not a long time!).

The Team

I was very fortunate to have worked with the talented individuals on this project. The team was comprised of 5 members in total, one member of which was responsible for general management and scrum. Another two were responsible for art assets and visual design/art direction. Finally, another member and I were assigned software engineering responsibilities. Notably; I also took on the role of Game Designer.

The Client

Without revealing too much, (due to NDA), the client was an individual interested in making a nutritionally educational game; primarily focused towards youth-audiences. We worked closely with them, on a weekly-basis for meetings, ensuring our product was headed towards their specific vision.

My Process

Weeks 1 & 2

The beginning weeks were dedicated to ideation and design, so I started researching the clients work aswell as the resources given. After some meetings and my previous research, I begun to form a vision of what the client wanted. Which at the time; was essentially a remaster of the original game format, with new mechanics and game flow. After communicating with the team they seemed to be onboard with the direction.

Weeks 3 & 4

During these weeks, I discovered that my design and vision was not on par with the client. So I made a quick pivot into another design, which was further into the now mini-game system. In the next meeting, the client and team were onboard, and understood the vision alot better, (Something I realised was a weakness from the previous week). After this, we entered the development stage, so the other developer and I started getting some working prototypes. By the end of week 4, we had a great prototype built for two out of four of the mini-games.

Weeks 5 & 6

During the final weeks, the client was largely happy with what we had been building, (aside from some minor notes of course). So we kept building the final two mini-games, and we ended out with some really cool work. It ended up as a really cohesive 2 player experience, partly due to the work that I had done stringing the game flow together behind the scenes.

Reflections

This project was certainly a great exercise in working as a team and working under a scrum/agile based framework. I found that having tasks laid out clearly in sprints helped alot with my overall productivity and time-management. I also discovered a facet of game development that I really enjoyed, that being overall game design and direction.
